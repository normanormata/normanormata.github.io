{%- comment -%} Sidebar order comes from each document's `nav_order`, sorted explicitly here. It cannot come from collection order: GitHub Pages builds with Jekyll 3.10, which has no ordering mechanism for collections and falls back to date-then-path — which is why the live sidebar used to open with the Confession and then run alphabetically (Apostles', Athanasian, Book of Discipline, Belgic…). Jekyll 4 does support an `order:` list in _config.yml, so relying on that would look correct locally and stay broken in production. A group heading is printed each time `category` changes, so nav_order must keep documents of the same category contiguous. Headings come from site.nav_group_titles; an unrecognised category falls back to "Other" rather than silently dropping the document. Note `site.pages` here is the _pages *collection*, not Jekyll's built-in page list: Jekyll::Drops::SiteDrop#[] resolves collection labels ahead of its own `pages` delegate. Renaming the collection would change this. toc.html is passed sanitize=true so entry labels are stripped to plain text. Question headings carry inline markup — for the MESV toggle, and a couple of Heidelberg questions carry a proof marker — and unsanitised that markup lands inside the sidebar link. The theme styles `.book-summary ul.summary li span` as display:block with padding, so a variant word rendered on its own indented line; and a proof marker's nested inside the entry's made the parser split the entry into two links, one pointing at the proofs callout. {%- endcomment -%}
